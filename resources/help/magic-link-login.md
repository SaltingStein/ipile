If your site allows it, you can sign in using a link sent to your account's email address instead of a password. This works for existing accounts only; it does not create a new account.

## Request a link

1. Open **Sign in**.
2. Under **Sign in by email link**, enter **Email for sign-in link**.
3. Choose **Email me a sign-in link**.
4. Check your inbox and spam folder. Open the **Sign in** button in the email before the link expires.
5. If asked, enter your authenticator code to finish signing in. Your normal two-factor rules and trusted-device choices still apply.

The page always shows the same confirmation, whether or not the address has an eligible account. Only active accounts with an email address receive a link. Guest accounts do not receive one.

## Keep your link private

Anyone with the link may start signing in to your account. Do not forward or share it. If you did not request the email, ignore it.

Each link works once. Requesting another link replaces all earlier links. The usual expiry is **15 minutes**, but your site may choose a different period; the email tells you how long you have. Changing your account's email address also stops an older link from working.

## If you cannot sign in

- **No email arrives:** check the address, inbox and spam folder. Wait for email delivery before requesting another link. If it still does not arrive, contact an administrator.
- **The link is invalid, expired or already used:** return to **Sign in** and request a new link. Use the newest email.
- **Too many requests:** wait before trying again. You can request up to three links per hour for the same email from the same network. Busy shared networks may have an additional short limit.
- **The email-link form is missing:** your administrator has switched the option off. Use your password or another available sign-in method.
- **Your account is suspended:** contact an administrator. A sign-in link cannot restore a suspended account.

Closing new registrations does not prevent existing accounts from using sign-in links. Turning email-link sign-in off prevents links from working while it is off.
